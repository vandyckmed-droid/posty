    Method, universe and sources

    The score

    Fixed, with nothing to configure. Daily log returns on split- and dividend-adjusted closes. Each window stops 21 trading days short of today — the "−1" — because the most recent month tends to reverse rather than persist.

    • 12−1 = trading days t−252 to t−21, 231 returns
    • 6−1 = trading days t−126 to t−21, 105 returns

    Each window's score is its annualized return divided by its own annualized sigma (stdev × √252). The two are blended 50/50. Both legs use the same window conventions as the wider system.

    The chart on each row shows the whole 12 months including the skipped final month, so you can see what the score deliberately ignores. The shaded tail is that skipped month.

    Universe

    It is hardwired: nothing is added, substituted or screened out. Liquidity is measured and shown but is not a filter — several names here trade under $1M a day and would be hard to use in size. Check the figure before acting on a rank.

    Per-fund detail

    Tap any row. Holdings and their weights come from the fund's own Form N-PORT filing with the SEC — public, free and authoritative, but quarterly and published around 60 days in arrears, so the reporting date is printed above every list. Positions are filtered to operating companies; a fund's largest line is often a cash vehicle, and the share genuinely held in shares is reported separately.

    Sector and country weights, fee, fund size and issuer come from the price vendor's fund profile. Closest relative is the one other fund in this universe that moves most like it once the market's common movement is removed — worth checking before holding two rows that look like different bets.

    Reading it honestly

    A score is an estimate from a sample of daily returns, so it carries real uncertainty — roughly ±1.7 on the 12−1 leg and ±2.6 on the 6−1 leg at 90% confidence. Gaps of a tenth or two are noise. Momentum rank is a starting point for work, not a recommendation, and says nothing about what happens next.
